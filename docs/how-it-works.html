How house scanning works

For new teammates and agents. 26 September 2026. About ten minutes.

A homeowner scans the wall around their electric meter with an iPhone. The server checks Base Power's placement rules on a 3D model of that wall. The app shows where the battery goes, in AR.

Think of a home inspector with a tape measure and a checklist. The phone is the eyes and the tape, and the server holds the checklist. One difference shapes the design: an inspector can lean around a bush, but the server can judge only what the phone saw.

1. Where the battery can go

The battery, 31 × 22 × 39.5 in, sits on the outside wall near the meter, because a cable runs between them. Today a person judges the fit from customer photos, which have no scale and miss what's just out of frame.

3. Four ideas that keep the answer honest

Error bars decide the answer

The phone tracks itself by dead reckoning, like counting steps, so its error grows with distance.

Unseen means unsure

Haze means unexplored, not empty, like fog of war in a strategy game.

Coverage is a fixed zone

Not the whole house, just a zone around the meter. The app always knows exactly what's left.

LiDAR helps but isn't required

LiDAR measures depth directly, but most homeowners' phones don't have it. Every check must work without it.

4. Building the 3D model

Still open. The server team is comparing three approaches on laser-scanned buildings, where the true shape is known.

Traditional reconstruction + LiDAR

Match points across photos, place them in 3D, and merge them into one surface. LiDAR depth drops straight in.

Weak on blank siding, and needs LiDAR or the phone's poses for scale.

Evidence: walls within 2.1 in (p90), with a laser scan standing in for LiDAR.

Learned depth + phone poses

A neural network guesses depth in each photo. Its scale is 4 to 12% off, so the phone's poses rescale it.

Evidence: walls about 5 in (p90). Edges 8 in or worse.

World models

Big models that turn photos or video into a whole 3D scene.

They fill in what nobody saw, and a check must know what was observed.

Evidence: not tested yet.

What a path must deliver

  • Real scale. 4 to 12% off is 5 to 14 in over a 10 ft wall.
  • Sharp edges. Clearances are measured from edges.
  • A record of what it saw. Filled-in wall must be marked.
  • It runs on the real packet, including phones without LiDAR.

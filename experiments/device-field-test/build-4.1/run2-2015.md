### A41-2015-02 (P1, app + server): the spot is on the AC stand-in

**What happened.**
- 3:46–3:48, "Tap the AC unit": the reticle sat on the folding table's near-left corner, beside the existing Base
  battery (`hi/ac_06.jpg`). The table stands in the corner and runs from there to the wooden right wall.
- `scene.json` object: `ac`, `span_ft` 13.11–14.10, a footprint of about 0.98 ft × 0.98 ft, 0.5–1.7 ft out from the wall.
- Result: spot **17.10 → 19.68 ft**, exactly 3 ft 0 in past the AC square (the AC rule's minimum). That stretch is under
  the right half of the table, the thing the tester called the AC unit.
- The earlier run tapped the same table in its middle and got an AC at 16.7–17.7 ft. So where the one tap lands decides
  where the whole unit is.

**Why the spot went to the far end** (from `server_rerun.json`, 784 candidates, 0 pass, 654 unsure, 130 fail):
- `_rank_unsure` (`solver.py:1208`) ranks candidates whose best estimate breaks a rule below all others (`estimate_fails`).
- Starts from 2.4 to ~10.3 ft are within 3 ft of the window (5.43–7.33 ft) on the estimate. Starts from ~7.5 to 17.1 ft
  are within 3 ft of the AC square.
- The only start whose estimates all clear is 17.1 ft: past the AC, with 17 ft of cable.
- So the single-tap AC and the unmarkable existing battery (#27) drive the whole answer.

**Fix direction.**
- App: mark an AC (and other boxy equipment) with **two taps** (left and right edges, as for windows), or at least a
  realistic default footprint (a condenser is ~3 ft wide) centred on the tap, and say so.
- App: add "Another battery / other equipment" as a mark kind (#27).
- Server: until footprints are real, treat a single-point object as having a size error of at least half a typical unit.

### A41-2015-03 (P1, app): the left end snapped to the meter

**What happened** (`hi/ev_left_end_at_meter.jpg`):
- 1:37.9–1:46.4, "Walk slowly to your left". The tester walked to the post. The strip showed amber cells for ~1–4 ft
  left of the meter.
- 1:45.5, **"Wall ends here"**, then "What's at the left end?", then "Something blocks it". The strip redrew with the
  meter at its left edge, and every cell left of the meter was gone. The AR end pole was drawn in the turf in line with
  the meter, not at the post.
- Packet: mark `wall_end_left`, `end_kind: limit`, at s = **0.0 m**. `scene.json` baseline starts 0.33 ft left of the
  meter. `scene.coverage.observed` still has ground from −4.5 ft, seen from the right side.

**Root cause.**
- `endWallHere` → `walkedEnd(.left)` → `WalkedEnd.end` (`HouseScanKit/.../Guidance/WalkedEnd.swift`):
  `side.sign * (along < 0 ? reach : min(along, reach))`, where `reach` = the farthest *kept photo position* on that side.
- Kept photo positions projected on the wall (scene frame): **no kept photo has s < 0 in the whole run.** The two photos
  during the left walk were at s = +1.06 ft (1:37.8) and +0.35 ft (1:43.3). Auto-capture keeps one every ~0.5 m moved,
  and the phone moved less than that to the left.
- So `reach` = 0, and the end is clamped to the meter whatever the phone saw.

**Relation to #24.** The #24 fix moved the end from "unbroken coverage" to "kept photo positions" so that ends stop
collapsing onto the meter. Short sides still collapse, because a kept photo needs 0.5 m of movement.

**Fix direction.**
- Take the maximum of: the phone's own position, the farthest kept photo, and the farthest wall cell *seen* on that side.
- Or keep a photo when "Wall ends here" is tapped.
- Never silently drop cells the strip was showing a second earlier.

**Impact here:** small. The post is close to the meter and the end is a `limit`. On a real wall with a few feet to the
left of the meter, a candidate side would be lost.

  It reads as a different request, not as "change position".

**Fix.**
- When the row has one sighting and the phone hasn't moved, say so: "Now take one step to the side and look again".
  The planner knows this: one sighting, and the current position within 25 cm of it.
- Merge step-back into the same card.
- Timeout or skip as in A41-1-03.
- Consider centring the task on where the phone stands rather than ±0.30 m around the meter.

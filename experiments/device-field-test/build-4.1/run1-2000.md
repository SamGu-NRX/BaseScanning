### A41-1-01 (P0, app): ground height taken from a `table` plane

**What happened.**
- `session.meter_anchor.ground_y_m` is −0.5146 m.
- The packet's `planes` include a `floor` plane at y = −1.474 m (11.2 m × 8.0 m) and a `table` plane at
  y = −0.516 m, 1.4 m out from the wall, with a 1.0 m × 5.9 m extent.
- `ground_y_m` equals the table plane's height to the millimetre.

**Effects** (1 m ≈ 3 ft 3 in; the offset is 0.96 m = 3 ft 2 in):
- `scene.meter.pos` y = 1.69 ft, so the meter is exported 1 ft 8 in above "ground". The real height is about
  4 ft 10 in.
- The window is exported at 0.19–4.06 ft. The real height is about 3.3–7.2 ft.
- Every ground sample row (0, 2 and 4 ft out) sits in mid-air, 3 ft above the turf. The foot-of-wall row is a
  point **on the wall** below the meter. That's why the target ring sits on the wall and the post
  (3:24–4:11, `hi/ev_tilt_arrow.jpg` frame 4).
- The arrow says **tilt up** while the card says "Tilt down" (3:44–3:46): the target is above the camera's
  view of the turf.
- The ground coverage row never goes green. The strip's ground row stayed amber for the whole walk.
  `scene.coverage.observed` has ground only at 0–1 ft and 10–11.5 ft.
- The gap patches ("about 11 ft right", "15 ft from the wall") are drawn at the folding table's height; at
  4:44–4:47 the patch lies exactly on the tabletop. At 5:58–5:59 the patch at the meter is drawn at the
  bottom of the conduit, not on the turf.
- The AR battery, had it rendered, would stand on that plane, about 3 ft above the turf (inferred from
  `ResultARModel.build`, which puts the unit at height 0 = `groundY`).
- Server heights are all shifted: the headroom and "box above" bands (6.5 ft up) and window heights for the
  opening check. The overhead tilt target (`tiltUpAbove`) is too low by 3 ft.

**Root cause.**
- `groundBelow` (`ios/HouseScan/Runtime/ScanEngine+Actions.swift:57-66`) returns
  `near.map(\.y).max()`, the **highest** horizontal plane that is ≥ 0.3 m below the meter and within 2 m
  (plane centre distance minus its half-diagonal).
- `LiveCapture` passes every horizontal `ARPlaneAnchor` with no `classification` filter
  (`ios/HouseScan/Runtime/LiveCapture.swift:406-413`).
- A 5.9 m-long plane has a 3 m "radius", so the 2 m test barely constrains it.
- `refineGround` (`ScanEngine.swift:364-379`) re-runs this whenever the plane set changes, and lets a
  *higher* plane replace a lower one ("a better plane replaces an earlier one").

**Fix direction.**
- Take only `.floor` planes, or `.none` where classification isn't supported; drop `.table`, `.seat`,
  `.ceiling` and so on.
- Prefer the **lowest** plausible plane, or the plane under the phone's own position.
- Never let a later plane *raise* the ground by more than ~10 cm once a floor exists.
- Add a sanity band for meter height: 3–6.5 ft above the ground. Outside it, keep the guess and set
  `groundMeasured = false`.
- Log the ground's history in the packet: its source plane id and classification.

**Also note.** With LiDAR, runs 1–2 probably had depth or mesh ground and a detected floor early (inferred).
Without LiDAR, planes arrive late and a table can come first. This is the non-LiDAR path's first real test.

### A41-1-02 (P0, app): AR view draws nothing

**What happened.**
- 6:09 "See it on your wall", then 6:10–6:25 the live camera with "The spot an installer will check / 12 ft 3 in
  right of your meter, 13 ft of cable".
- Nothing is drawn in any frame at 2 fps (`hi/ar_sheet.jpg`, `hi/ev_ar_empty.jpg`). That includes frames
  aimed at the meter and conduit, where the cable should start, and at the 10–14 ft stretch.
- The card shows the spot's text, not "Point at your meter". So `state.tracking == .normal` and the result
  has a spot (`ResultARScreen.swift:37-48`).

**Code path (new in 4.1, #41 / `2d33cea`).**
- `ScanEngine.showResultInCamera` builds `ResultARModel` and calls `LiveCapture.showResult`, which hangs it
  on an `AnchorEntity(.anchor(identifier:))` for the meter anchor, with
  `model.orientation = simd_quatf(anchor.transform).inverse`. It then turns on occlusion.
- On an iPhone 14 that means re-running the session with `.personSegmentationWithDepth`
  (`LiveCapture.swift:130-170`).
- The SwiftUI Canvas fallback draws only when `!state.resultInCamera` (`ResultARScreen.swift:17`), so once
  `showResult` returns true there's no fallback.

**Hypotheses, for whoever debugs on a device.** Nothing below is confirmed.
- (a) Re-running the session for people occlusion drops or re-identifies the meter anchor, so the
  `AnchorEntity` never resolves.
- (b) The entity is enabled but placed off-screen: a wrong orientation or position composition, or the
  anchor's transform is not the frame `wall.meter` uses.
- (c) `setResultVisible(false)` was applied during a brief non-normal tracking state caused by the session
  re-run, and never re-enabled. `ingest` re-enables only when `frame.tracking` changes, and in `.idle` mode
  frames may not be delivered.
- (d) People occlusion hides everything: the whole view segmented as a person or with depth in front.

**Fix direction.**
- Check `RuntimeLog` and whether `showResult` returned true.
- Keep the Canvas overlay as a fallback until the RealityKit entity is confirmed on screen.
- Don't re-run the session on the AR screen; enable people occlusion from the start.
- Add a UI test hook.
- Independently of this, A41-1-01 would put the battery 3 ft up.

### A41-1-09 (P1, server): drift makes far spots unresolvable

The server re-run gives spot s = 10.96–13.54 ft. The unsure checks:
- AC: 3 ft 2 in ± 5 ft 7 in against a 3 ft rule.
- Window: 3 ft 0 in ± 4 ft 0 in.
- Cable run: 12 ft 7 in ± 6 ft 5 in against the 15 ft reach.

Where the errors come from:
- `errors.drift_per_ft` = 0.16 ft per ft from the meter (`server/rules.yaml:38-40`), from ADVIO walks on a
  2018 iPhone 6s with ARKit 1.0.
- The object's error and the wall's error at the spot are **added** (`solver.py:465`), although both come from
  the same walk and are strongly correlated.
- Result: 825 candidates, 0 pass, 719 unsure, 106 fail. No spot more than ~5 ft from the meter can pass a 3 ft
  clearance, so every scan without a spot beside the meter ends in `manual_review`.

**Fix direction.**
- Re-estimate drift on current phones: the field tape test, or the packet's trajectory against the tape.
- Treat errors of features measured in the same walk as relative, not summed absolutes.
- Consider measuring clearances between two features locally, from the photos that see both.
